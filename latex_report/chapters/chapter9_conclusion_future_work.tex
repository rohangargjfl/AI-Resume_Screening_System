\chapter{CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK}

\section{Conclusion}
The use of Artificial Intelligence in recruitment has moved past being merely an academic endeavor; it is a necessary step for businesses to take because of how many resumes companies receive today. However, the implementation of huge deep learning systems at the expense of transparency is not feasible for most organizations. This B.Tech Project-II presents a lightweight yet explainable AI prototype capable of resume screening in practice.

By leveraging the power of layout-free document parsing, deterministic Regex skill extraction, and TF-IDF scoring, our approach proves itself to be very practical and commercializable. Our system puts emphasis on achieving lightning-fast processing speeds and complete textual transparency rather than trying to understand every word's meaning accurately. The usage of bounding algorithms effectively mitigates keyword stuffing abuse cases, and the Explainable AI component acts as a middle ground between the complex mathematical score vector and human HR professionals. Overall, this system is envisioned not as an alternative to human intuition, but rather as a powerful yet affordable tool for initial decision-making under the limitations of SME hardware.


\section{Future Work}
There is room for several concrete improvements to turn the prototype into a full-fledged enterprise-grade product:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hybrid Context Module:} Future versions should attempt to merge Approach A and Approach B into a unified solution. Specifically, the regex shield can be executed first, followed by the Sentence-BERT engine only if the score falls within the range of 45\%--55\%. This approach would optimize the overall computational cost while improving semantic understanding.
    
    \item \textbf{Dynamic Skills Ontology using Knowledge Graphs:} Instead of manually maintaining a dictionary of skills, the system could leverage a dynamic ontology powered by knowledge graphs updated with new information through APIs from platforms such as LinkedIn or StackOverflow.
    
    \item \textbf{Fairness and Bias Auditor:} Debiasing algorithms should be implemented to remove demographic information such as names, gender, cities, and university affiliations before the resume enters the mathematical processing pipeline.
    
    \item \textbf{Monitoring of Models:} Continuous monitoring mechanisms should be introduced to track the distribution of scores generated by the TF-IDF model and identify any unintended preference toward particular keywords.
    
    \item \textbf{Feedback Loop for Recruiters:} Human recruiters should be provided with the ability to tag ``false positive'' and ``false negative'' decisions on the dashboard, enabling the AI system to gradually update the internal vector weights of the model.
\end{itemize}
