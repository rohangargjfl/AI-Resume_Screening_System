\chapter{INTRODUCTION}

\section{Background of AI in Recruitment}
The human resource (HR) industry has undergone a massive digital transformation over the past decade. The proliferation of online job portals, decentralized remote work opportunities, and professional networking platforms has fundamentally democratized job hunting. Candidates can now apply for positions globally with a single click. While this exponentially expands the talent pool available to organizations, it simultaneously inundates HR departments with an unmanageable volume of applications. It is highly common for a single open position at a mid-sized technology firm to attract hundreds, if not thousands, of resumes within hours of posting. To manage this influx, organizations have increasingly turned to digital Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) to automate the initial phases of the hiring funnel. Recently, Artificial Intelligence (AI) has been heavily integrated into these systems to transition them from simple digital filing cabinets into intelligent screening agents capable of analyzing textual data and ranking candidates based on their predicted likelihood of success.

\section{Problems in Manual Resume Screening}
The traditional approach of manual resume screening is plagued by three fundamental challenges: time consumption, resource exhaustion, and cognitive bias. HR professionals are frequently tasked with reviewing massive applicant pipelines, often spending mere seconds scanning a single resume. In this constrained timeframe, human reviewers look for visual cues, recognizable university names, or specific former employers, which severely limits the depth and fairness of the evaluation. This rapid cognitive processing inevitably introduces human biases, ranging from demographic prejudices to ``layout bias'' or ``format bias''. In layout bias scenarios, a candidate is judged favorably simply because their resume possesses an aesthetically pleasing graphical format, or conversely, penalized because a dense layout appears overwhelming. Furthermore, the financial overhead of maintaining massive recruiting teams dedicated solely to first-pass filtering is entirely unsustainable for Small and Medium-sized Enterprises (SMEs).

\section{The Limitations of Keyword Matching}
The first generation of automated screening tools relied entirely on rigid boolean keyword matching. If a Job Description (JD) required ``Python'' and ``Data Analysis,'' the ATS would blindly search the candidate's uploaded document for those exact character strings. This approach suffers from severe limitations that actively harm the recruitment pipeline. First, it completely ignores the ``Implicit Competency'' gap; a candidate who writes that they ``architected scalable backend web systems'' possesses strong engineering skills but will likely be rejected if the ATS is explicitly looking for the exact phrase ``backend developer''. Second, primitive keyword matching is easily manipulated by ``keyword stuffing''. Keyword stuffing is an adversarial practice where applicants paste the entire job description into the margins of their resume in hidden, white-colored text to trick the parser into assigning a perfect 100\% match score.

\section{The Need for Explainable and Cost-Efficient Screening}
As the industry recognized the inherent flaws of legacy keyword systems, the pendulum swung rapidly toward highly complex deep learning models, utilizing architectures such as BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers) and Large Language Models (LLMs) to perform semantic matching. While these models resolve the keyword mismatch problem by understanding the contextual similarity between ``REST'' and ``RESTful,'' they introduce two critical new problems: astronomical computational costs and a complete lack of transparency. Commercial enterprise AI tools often act as ``black boxes''. When a deep neural network ranks a candidate at a 92\% match, the recruiter has no mathematical or logical way to explain exactly why that specific score was generated. If a rejected candidate demands justification, or files a discrimination lawsuit, the organization cannot audit the black-box neural network to prove that the decision was entirely merit-based and free from protected-class bias. Consequently, there is a pressing market need for AI screening tools that are highly transparent, auditable, and lightweight enough to run cost-effectively without requiring expensive, dedicated cloud GPU clusters.

\section{Motivation for the Project}
The core motivation for this project stems from the observation that while hiring is becoming heavily automated through technology, the tools available to SMEs are either too rudimentary (legacy ATS) or too resource-intensive and opaque (LLMs) to be deployed safely and efficiently. The hiring process remains resource-intensive, and manually reviewing resumes leads to inconsistent selections. Therefore, there is a distinct necessity to engineer a structured approach that improves upon keyword matching through intelligent parsing and mathematical bounds, while avoiding the heavy computational overhead of generative AI. The motivation is to construct a system that empowers HR professionals with data-driven insights rather than replacing them with an inscrutable algorithmic oracle.

\section{System Boundaries and Functional Constraints}
It is paramount to frame the functional boundaries of this project accurately to prevent overclaiming. The developed system is not presented as a flawless, heavy commercial AI product capable of deep semantic reasoning, nor is it the ultimate solution to the highly complex sociological challenge of human talent acquisition. It is not designed to replace comprehensive human interviews. Instead, the project delivers a lightweight, explainable, low-cost first-pass screening prototype. It explicitly validates a practical engineering trade-off: trading the deep semantic nuance of massive transformer models for blazing-fast speed, absolute deterministic explainability, and near-zero server infrastructure costs. It is engineered specifically for constrained hardware and small-to-medium-scale recruitment workflows.

\section{Problem Statement}
Because organizations are subjected to an overwhelming volume of resumes for every job posting, the hiring process is both time-consuming and resource-intensive, and manually reviewing resumes to screen candidates leads to inconsistent, biased selections. Current accessible AI tools still heavily rely on rigid keyword searches that fail to accurately reflect a candidate's contextual competencies, while state-of-the-art semantic AI tools are computationally prohibitive and operate as unexplainable black boxes. Thus, there is a critical need for an automated system that bridges this gap by providing fast, cost-efficient, and fully explainable candidate matching.
