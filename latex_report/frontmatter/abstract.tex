\chapter*{ABSTRACT}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

The recruiting industry has significant structure in dealing with a high number of applications which are usually unstructured in nature for each position. Recruiting online is becoming increasingly complex due to an increase in the number of applications. These applications can be screened manually by recruiters which is not only costly and time-consuming but also prone to error and unconscious discrimination. Given the lack of time, recruiters may screen candidates based on visual appearance, recognizable institutions, or even company familiarity than analyzing their relevance with the position.

In that regard, automated applicant tracking systems can save costs and time but most systems are still focused on keyword-based matching. The keyword-based approach lacks context and fails to consider the implicit competencies of applicants while other models like LLMs and sentence embedding techniques like Sentence-BERT are more semantically rich but require a lot of resources and compute time and also run as black-box systems that are hard to audit. This project tries to find out how AI can be used strategically within resume analysis software in order to optimize the trade-off between effectiveness and efficiency of matching, as well as transparency.

It is neither aimed at producing a complete commercial product nor building an optimal AI algorithm. Instead, we are developing a lightweight, transparent and affordable tool for first-pass screening and decision making in an environment with limited hardware capabilities and SME business cases. The project's workflow includes several lightweight AI algorithms and techniques as well as some software engineering tools. Candidates' resumes and positions' descriptions are provided via the Flask-based website. Parsing resumes can take digital PDF, DOCX, TXT and image-based resumes.

Once text extraction is completed, NLP is conducted through SpaCy and regular expressions (regex). Both JD and candidate resumes are subjected to NLP which extracts technical skills, soft skills, and number of years of experience. Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF) vectorization with Cosine Similarity was used to evaluate contextual similarity. The system calculates a custom formula which incorporates all four metrics and provides a weighted score. The upper bounds on scores are applied in case the candidate possesses too many skills or experience beyond the job requirements in order to avoid distorted results.

In order to solve algorithmic opacity problems and build an explainable AI, a custom module called the Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) is added to the system. Instead of generating only the final percentage, the system provides details about skill match, missing skills, additional skills, experience match, and score breakdown by component.

This prototype can be viewed as a tool that assists recruiters instead of selecting candidates autonomously since it is designed in a way that ensures full transparency.

A number of benchmarking tests were carried out to examine different prototypes' performance and compare design architectures. Benchmarking 1 explored format bias and OCR failure tolerance through TXT, PDF, and PNG inputs. Benchmarking 2 tested resilience against keyword stuffing and content dilution. Benchmarking 3 explored the output produced by Explainable AI and calculated mathematical boundary conditions. Benchmarking 4 compares the proposed prototype based on Regex + TF-IDF model (Approach A), purely semantic approach using Sentence-BERT/LLM (Approach C) and hybrid future approach (Approach B) where semantic and lexical matching approaches are combined. Benchmarking 5 measures efficiency, scalability, and cost when applied to 100 resumes.

Qualitative results show clear pros and cons of each solution but also indicate a tradeoff. While LLM models perform better in terms of understanding semantics, such as recognizing ``REST'' and ``RESTful'' to refer to the same concept, the current prototype achieves millisecond processing time with minimal memory consumption of only 0.12 MB. On the other hand, the semantic Sentence-BERT/LLM takes 4.1860 seconds and 7.33 MB memory. Hybrid Approach takes 3.7217 seconds and 1.35 MB of memory. Therefore, the prototype is less efficient semantically but faster in terms of execution speed.

In conclusion, the project has proven the efficiency of the transparent decision support system which increases shortlisting speed yet keeps control in hands of a person.
